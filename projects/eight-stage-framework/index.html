Project 01 • Curriculum

Eight-Stage Lesson Framework

A standardized architecture for classroom-ready 80-minute university English lessons.

Challenge

Early lesson materials varied in pacing, completeness, formatting, and teacher support. Some finished early, forcing instructors to improvise. Answer keys, instructions, audio, and visual guidance were inconsistent.

Design response

I concluded that an 80-minute lesson needed approximately eight stages of about ten minutes each. The framework creates a predictable rhythm among teacher input, student practice, interaction, independent work, and feedback.

How it works

  1. Define the outcome and CEFR-appropriate language target.
  2. Sequence approximately eight stages across the available time.
  3. Prepare activities, teacher guidance, answers, visuals, and audio.
  4. Teach the lesson and observe pacing, clarity, and difficulty.
  5. Revise the source lesson for the next class or semester.

A complete eight-stage example

This example comes from an anonymized lesson on city facilities and change. The activity types vary by lesson, but the timing and progression remain consistent.

01Picture vocabulary10 minutes
02Directions vocabulary10 minutes
03Frequency practice10 minutes
04Reading10 minutes
05Grammar in context10 minutes
06Guided writing10 minutes
07Information-gap speaking10 minutes
08Proposal and production10 minutes

Evidence from the print-ready team file

These pages come from the completed 49-page Unit 6: Places, Facilities, and Energy file used by the teaching team and prepared for student printing. They show the final formatting, upgraded illustrations, classroom activities, and parallel teacher answers.

What this evidence demonstrates

One lesson includes a timed sequence, visual anchors, vocabulary, receptive work, controlled practice, guided interaction, independent production, and a prepared answer key. The finished unit contains three lessons, 24 stages, and dedicated teacher-answer pages.

Reach and reflection

The framework supports approximately 60 lessons per semester and 120 lessons per academic year. Its verified value is operational and pedagogical consistency; formal comparative learning-outcome data has not yet been collected.

Lesson learned

The best curriculum architecture is specific enough to create consistency but flexible enough to support different aims and activity types.
