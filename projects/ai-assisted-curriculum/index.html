Project 03 • AI & Curriculum

AI-Assisted Curriculum Development

A human-controlled workflow that turns AI-assisted drafts into CEFR-appropriate, illustrated, audio-supported, teacher-ready university lessons.

Challenge

AI can accelerate a first draft, but a first draft is not a curriculum. Early outputs often used language above the students’ CEFR level and could be inconsistent, incomplete, or difficult to teach. A classroom-ready lesson also needs clear sequencing, usable instructions, student activities, visual support, audio where appropriate, and complete answers for the teacher.

Design response

I developed a human-controlled production workflow around the Eight-Stage Lesson Framework. AI supports drafting and asset creation, while I retain responsibility for level, pedagogy, accuracy, formatting, and classroom usability. Each stage is built around a specific student action and then reviewed as part of the complete 80-minute lesson.

Quality-controlled workflow

01DefineSet the outcome, CEFR range, and student action.
02GenerateCreate a constrained draft for the individual stage.
03CheckReview language level, accuracy, instructions, and cognitive load.
04CompleteAdd the activity, image, audio, answers, and teacher support.
05TeachObserve timing, comprehension, engagement, and usability.
06ReviseImprove the shared source so the next class benefits.

Evidence from a production lesson unit

The examples below come from the final, team-accessible version of Unit 5: Finding Your Way. The 53-page unit contains three lessons, 24 student-facing stages, a full teacher answer-key section, and two ElevenLabs audio files. It is the version colleagues can access during teaching and the version prepared for student printing.

The final product is not raw AI output.

It combines instructional decisions, CEFR-level editing, visual consistency, audio production, document formatting, answer preparation, and repeated classroom revision.

Human quality gates

  • CEFR control: vocabulary, grammar, and instructions must be accessible to the target learners.
  • Accuracy: facts, maps, directions, examples, transcripts, and answers must agree.
  • Teachability: each stage must support the intended interaction and fit the lesson timing.
  • Answerability: every closed task needs an unambiguous answer or useful model response.
  • Teacher usability: materials should be ready to teach without unnecessary preparation.
  • Print readability: layout, contrast, spacing, and image detail must survive classroom printing.

Reach and limits

This workflow supports the continuing development and refinement of approximately 60 lessons per semester, or 120 lessons in an academic year. The ElevenLabs production approach has also been adopted by other instructors. The demonstrated result is a faster, more consistent production process; I do not claim a measured improvement in student outcomes without formal comparative data.

Lesson learned

Generation speed matters only when a disciplined review process turns the output into accurate, level-appropriate, classroom-ready material. AI assists; the curriculum designer remains accountable.
