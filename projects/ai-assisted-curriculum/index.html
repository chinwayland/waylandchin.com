Project 03 • AI & Curriculum

AI-Assisted Curriculum Development

Faster material production with human control over level, pedagogy, accuracy, and usability.

Challenge

Initial AI output often used language above the students’ CEFR level and still required formatting, sequencing, answer keys, visuals, audio, and classroom testing.

Design response

I refined prompts and editing practices to constrain student-facing language, then standardized a workflow around the eight-stage framework. Stage-specific black-and-white illustrations provide visual anchors, while ElevenLabs supports consistent audio.

Quality-controlled workflow

  1. Define the outcome, CEFR range, stage, and student action.
  2. Generate a constrained first draft.
  3. Check vocabulary, grammar, instructions, and cognitive load.
  4. Create the worksheet, teacher guide, answer key, image, and audio.
  5. Teach, observe, and revise.

Reach and reflection

The workflow helps maintain a large lesson set without treating first-generation AI output as finished curriculum. Other instructors now use the shared audio workflow. The defensible impact is faster production with improved consistency.

Evidence to addPrompt-to-final case study, CEFR adaptation, representative illustrations, audio sample, and linked teacher guide and answer key.

Lesson learned

Faster production is valuable only when explicit quality gates keep the result accurate, teachable, level-appropriate, and usable by another teacher.

Principle

AI assists. Teachers decide.

Curriculum quality comes from decisions about language level, sequencing, context, and revision—not generation speed alone.
