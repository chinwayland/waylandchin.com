Project 07 • Faculty Support

Digital Enablement and Faculty Support

Helping colleagues reach shared teaching systems through the accounts, devices, and classroom computers they actually use.

The last mile of educational technology

A well-designed curriculum or digital system has little value if a teacher cannot open the files in the classroom. In a mixed-device environment, access can be interrupted by unfamiliar sign-in procedures, authentication requirements, missing software, limited hardware, or differences between personal phones and institutional computers.

My contribution has been practical and informal: identify the point of failure, create a workable path, test it in the real teaching environment, and remain available when the same workflow breaks again.

A problem-solving workflow

1Observe the failure

Start with what the colleague sees, not an assumed technical cause.

2Locate the barrier

Separate account, permission, authentication, software, device, and hardware problems.

3Create the shortest working path

Use the simplest reliable solution available in that environment.

4Test where teaching happens

Confirm access on the classroom computer or personal device that will actually be used.

5Strengthen the wider system

Use recurring support problems to improve shared instructions, files, or workflows.

From individual support to programme reliability

The support is often delivered one person and one device at a time, but its value extends to shared systems. Centralized attendance depends on instructors being able to reach the same workbook. Standardized lessons depend on reliable access to current teaching files. Calendar automation and other tools matter only when their outputs open on the devices colleagues use.

What this work demonstrates

  • Technology adoption: implementation includes helping real people cross the gap between a tool and dependable daily use.
  • Operational empathy: solutions begin with the colleague’s actual constraints rather than an ideal device or setup.
  • Persistence: access problems may require repeated support, alternative hardware, or a different route to the same resource.
  • Systems thinking: recurring individual problems reveal where the wider process needs clearer instructions or better design.
Scope and role

This case study describes informal technical and workflow support provided alongside my lecturer responsibilities. It does not imply a formal IT, management, or faculty-leadership title.

Reflection

Technology adoption is often harder than technology creation. The final measure of a system is not whether it works in a demonstration—it is whether colleagues can depend on it during a normal teaching day. That requires patient troubleshooting, realistic workarounds, and design choices that respect the environment already in place.

Privacy by design

No colleague names, account details, private teaching files, device identifiers, or authentication information are shown in this case study.
